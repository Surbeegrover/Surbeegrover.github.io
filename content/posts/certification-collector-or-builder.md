A new AI certification now shows up almost every month. GitHub, Anthropic, OpenAI and Google each have one, and most reviews rank them by difficulty.

That's the wrong question. The useful one is: **will this change how I work, or only how my profile looks?**

I've taken several, so I score each one twice. This is a living post. I update it every time I sit another one.

## The short version

| Certification | My verdict |
|---|---|
| GitHub Copilot (GH-300) | A cheap confirmation if you already use Copilot every day. |
| GitHub Agentic AI Developer (GH-600) | The one that changes how you build. The signal is still catching up. |
| Claude Architect track | Strongest on both scores, if your employer gets you in. |
| Claude Certified Associate | Solid fundamentals, same access catch. |
| OpenAI certificate courses | A free, structured on-ramp. Not a differentiator. |
| Google Generative AI Leader | I sit it November 21. Scores added right after. |

## How I score

- **Signal (out of 5):** how much a recruiter, client or manager reads from it.
- **Builder (out of 5):** how much it changes what you do on Monday.

The two often disagree, and the gap is the interesting part.

| | Low builder | High builder |
|---|---|---|
| **High signal** | **The Trophy.** Looks great, teaches little. | **The Unicorn.** Worth it either way. |
| **Low signal** | **The Dust Collector.** Skip. | **The Sleeper.** Nobody's impressed, you quietly get better. |

Three things stand out when you line them up.

**1. A certification is a subscription, not an ownership.** Most expire after a year. Google is the exception at three. Renewal is usually free and online, but a lapsed Claude certification means re-sitting the full exam at full price. If you collect five, you have five renewal dates to track. Put each one in your calendar the day you pass.

**2. Access is uneven.** The Claude exams are open only to people at Claude Partner Network organizations, registered with a company email. If your employer isn't a partner, you can't just sign up. Google has no prerequisites at all.

**3. The outlines show what each one values.** GH-300 is built around Copilot features and responsible use. GH-600 has six domains: architecture and the software lifecycle, tool use, memory and state, evaluation and error analysis, multi-agent orchestration, and guardrails and accountability. Read that list as a job description. It describes someone who governs agents, not someone who prompts them.

## One at a time

### GitHub Copilot (GH-300): Signal 3, Builder 2

I passed this after a few weeks of using Copilot every day. That is its strength and its limit. If you already work in the tool, the certification mostly confirms what you know. If you don't, preparing for it is a structured tour of the features you've been skipping.

**Take it if** you use Copilot already and want a quick, low-cost credential.
**Skip it if** you hope it will teach you to build with agents. It isn't aiming at that.

### GitHub Agentic AI Developer (GH-600): Signal 3, Builder 4

Proctored, 120 minutes, pass mark 700. The domains are why it scores high for builders. Evaluation, memory and state, orchestration and guardrails are the parts of agent work that demos skip and production punishes.

The signal score sits below the builder score on purpose. It's new, so the certification hasn't had time to become a familiar signal. If that changes, the score should rise, which makes it a sleeper for now.

**Take it if** you're putting agents into real delivery workflows and need the discipline of evals and guardrails.
**Skip it if** you only want a logo for your profile. The preparation is real work.

### Claude Certified Associate and the Architect track: 3/3 and 4/4

Anthropic offers four credentials: Associate, Developer Foundations, Architect Foundations and Architect Professional. All are closed-book (no notes, docs or AI) and run 120 minutes, with multiple choice plus scenario-based multiple-response questions.

The Associate covers everyday use well. The Architect credentials move to designing agent systems and, at the professional level, integration, governance and evaluation at scale. That is why I score them higher on both axes. Some of the signal comes from scarcity, because the partner requirement limits who can sit them.

**Take it if** you're at a partner organization and design or build on Claude.
**Skip it if** you can't register anyway, or you work mostly with other models. The free public learning material is still worth your time.

### OpenAI certificate courses: Signal 2, Builder 3

These are courses inside ChatGPT, currently a pilot with select employers. The fuller OpenAI Certification adds a hands-on project, which is what earns the builder score of 3. The signal is 2 because it isn't a proctored exam, so a recruiter can't easily tell who learned the material from who clicked through it.

**Take it if** you're new to AI and want a free, structured start.
**Skip it if** you need a credential that carries weight with a skeptical reader.

### Google Generative AI Leader: scores after November 21

I sit this one tomorrow, and I'll add scores and notes here right after. On paper it's the most accessible of the group: 90 minutes, 50 to 60 multiple-choice questions, $99, online or at a test center, no prerequisites, valid for three years. It covers generative AI fundamentals, Google Cloud's generative AI offerings, techniques for improving model output, and business strategy for generative AI.

It's written for any role, technical or not. I'm curious how it reads from the technical side of the table.

## Pick by goal

| If you are... | Start with | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Already using Copilot daily and want a quick win | GH-300 | Cheap and fast, and it confirms what you do |
| Putting agents into real delivery workflows | GH-600 | Evals, guardrails and orchestration are the point |
| Designing on Claude at a partner organization | Claude Architect track | Closest to real system design |
| A leader or non-technical and need AI fluency | Google Generative AI Leader | Broad, no prerequisites, three-year validity |
| New to AI and want a free, structured start | OpenAI certificate courses | Low cost, includes a hands-on project |

## How to prepare for any of them

I don't use or recommend "brain dump" practice material, and candidate agreements forbid sharing exam content, so nothing here describes questions. This is what works:

1. **Start from the official skills outline.** It's public, and it tells you what's measured.
2. **Turn every domain into something you do.** Don't just read about evals. Write a small one for something you built.
3. **Build one small, real thing per domain.** An afternoon each is enough.
4. **Write down what you got wrong, in your own words.** You'll keep it better than from re-reading.
5. **Schedule the renewal the day you pass.** It's a calendar entry that saves you from re-sitting the exam.

## What none of them measure

None of these certifications can tell you whether someone can:

- decide when *not* to use AI
- review AI-written work and catch what's wrong with it
- write down what "good" looks like before the model starts
- ship to production and keep it running
- explain a failure honestly and fix it

Here's a five-question self-check I trust more than any certificate:

1. Can you show a pull request where an agent's work was reviewed and a real problem was caught?
2. Do you have an eval that would fail if the agent got worse?
3. Do you know who approves what, and where that decision is recorded?
4. Could a new teammate understand your agent setup without asking you?
5. Have you thrown away an AI-generated approach because it wasn't good enough?

If you can say yes to most of those, a certification is a bonus. If you can't, it's a reasonable place to start learning, but it won't finish the job.
